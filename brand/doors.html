Behind one door

Rebuilt from Saint Haven's own front-door film. In it, the camera never moves and the door never opens. The only thing that changes is the light drifting across the stone. That's the motion language here: slow, eased, led by light, with nothing mechanical. All three variants use the real footage and enter through the real door.

What the reference does

  • A locked-off 37-second loop of the arched front door, muted
  • Sunlight and leaf shadow move across the facade, warming and cooling
  • No cuts, no camera moves, nothing that draws attention to itself

So the motion should

  • Move like a camera, not a UI. One slow push, never a swing or a slide.
  • Change light before it changes position, and dissolve rather than wipe.
  • Use the door's real arch as the only shape. Nothing drawn on top.

What I removed from round 1

  • Drawn lacquer doors, 3D swings, bronze glints and light beams
  • Curtains and hold-to-enter gates
  • Anything a visitor has to wait for before reading the headline

Choreography

0 to 0.5sThe front copy lifts away (opacity, 8px up). Nothing else moves yet.
0 to 2.1sThe camera walks forward: the film scales from 100% to 240%, centred on the door, with cubic-bezier(.45,0,.25,1). It's slow to start and slow to arrive, like a footstep.
0.2 to 1.1sThe hall dissolves into the door's arch, which is tracked exactly to the doorway in the film, so the glass becomes a view inside.
1.3 to 2.8sThe arch widens to full frame and flattens only at the very end. The hall settles from 118% to 100% with ease-out, like your eyes adjusting.
2.6s"Welcome to your sanctuary" comes into focus (opacity, 10px, 4px blur to 0, 900ms).

Feels like

Walking up to the door

One continuous camera move, the way a film would show arrival.

Why it works for conversion

Nothing is gated. Apply sits beside Step inside from the first frame, so curious visitors get a reward and ready visitors lose nothing.

Build

One GSAP timeline: a scale on the film wrapper and a clip-path on the interior layer, with the door position mapped from the video's cover crop. No 3D, no canvas.

The arch shape

The arch is cut to the real door's ratio (0.57), not a generic semicircle, so the page's shapes come from the building.

Motion spec for dev

Motion plan

  • Tier 1, interaction: buttons warm to ivory on hover in 200ms, and press to 98% in 160ms. Links underline from the centre out in 300ms.
  • Tier 2, reveals: sections rise 14px and fade in over 700ms with ease-out, once only, triggered at 20% visible. Lists are staggered 100ms apart. The hero headline is never delayed.
  • Tier 3, signature (one per page): the entrance, A, B or C. Nothing else on the page moves on its own apart from the reference film.

Technique

CSS keyframes and IntersectionObserver for reveals. GSAP with ScrollTrigger is used for the A timeline and B scrub, and it's already loaded on sainthaven.com.au. The door position is mapped from the film's cover crop in a resize handler, so the arch lines up at any screen size.

Assets and fallbacks

Uses the existing front-door loop from the live site (1280×716 delivered). Needs a still poster frame from the same file, and a high-resolution interior. The hall here is the guideline render at 1,500px, which softens at full width. Lazy-load the interior after first paint.

Reduced motion and slow connections

Reduced motion: poster frame, no camera walk, and "Step inside" scrolls to the next section. Slow connection or Save-Data: poster only, no video. Text is always live HTML, never baked into the film.

Anti-slop self-check

  • One signature motion per page: pass. A, B or C, never combined.
  • Identical fade-ups everywhere: pass. The hero uses focus-in, sections rise, and the arch opens from its base.
  • Looping decoration with no job: pass. The only loop is the client's own film. The scroll hint line is the one exception, and it disappears on first scroll.
  • Motion delays headline or CTA: pass. Both are visible from 0ms, and A's walk is optional.
  • Hard to build or heavy: pass. One timeline, transform, clip-path and opacity only. No 3D, no canvas.
  • Reduced motion handled: pass.
  • Synthetic-looking generated video: none used. Note that the front-door film is itself a render, so it needs to stay as subtle as it is now.

Recommendation

A, Step Inside, for the hero, with C's dusk and arch reveal underneath it. A gives the "gaining access" moment as an invitation rather than a gate, and C's scroll treatment carries the same film down the page without adding a second signature move, because it's only a parallax and a reveal.

Choose B instead if the page is brand-led rather than paid-traffic-led. You decide, and one round of changes is included.
